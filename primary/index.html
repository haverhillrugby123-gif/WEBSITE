When Primary tuition helps

Use tuition for a reason, not by default.

Primary support is most useful when the next goal is clear enough to teach and review.

Repair a persistent gapReading, writing, number or reasoning that is making later work harder.
Build independenceReduce reliance on prompts by moving from modelling to independent attempts.
Prepare for transitionConsolidate key skills before SATs or the move into secondary school where relevant.
Will Primary tuition just add more worksheets?

It should not. Tasks should be selected because they reveal or practise a specific skill, with discussion and feedback around the work.

Can lessons support both confidence and attainment?

Confidence is more useful when it grows from genuine competence. Clear explanations, achievable challenge and visible improvement can support both.

The teaching cycle

Primary tuition should reduce dependence, not create it.

The goal is not to make a child reliant on constant prompting. Good support gradually transfers more of the thinking back to the pupil.

01

Diagnose

Use current work, questioning and short tasks to find the real barrier.

02

Model

Make the hidden thinking visible: explain why a method works, not only what to write.

03

Practise

Move from supported examples to independent application and unfamiliar questions.

04

Respond to evidence

Use errors and successful work to decide what the pupil should do next.

A simple standard

A student should finish knowing something they did not know, or doing something they could not do, at the start.

That is a more useful measure of a lesson than how many pages were completed.
